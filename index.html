        Note

        How the number is made.

        Every item carries two layers. What the game says about it, and what it actually feels like to fight with. Only the second one can be argued about, and it is the only one written by hand.

        Two layers

        scraped comes out of items.xml and itempools.xml — quality, pools, item type, heart containers. It is never typed from memory, because a plausible-looking wrong quality number is worse than no quality number at all.

        rated is hand-assigned combat effectiveness across five axes. This is the part that cannot be scraped, and it is where the game's feel lives.

        The five axes

        AxisRangeMeaning
        offense0.5 – 3.0Effective DPS against one large target. 1.0 is neutral.
        aoe0.5 – 3.0Damage against adds and multiple targets. Drives the Beast fight.
        tracking0.0 – 1.0How reliably the build lands hits on an erratic, teleporting target. Drives Delirium.
        defense0.8 – 2.5Effective HP: containers, damage reduction, shields, on-hit heals.
        evasion0.0 – 1.0Mobility: flight, speed, range.

        A completely neutral item is 1.0 / 1.0 / 0.0 / 1.0 / 0.0. Sad Onion is the calibration anchor at 1.22 offense — if an item feels stronger than Sad Onion in a real run, its offense should exceed 1.22, and the gap should be proportional to how much stronger.

        Composing five items into one build

        • offense — multiply all five, then soft-cap at min(p, 3.5 + 0.4 × (p − 3.5)). Stops two Q4 damage items producing an auto-win.
        • aoe — multiply, same soft cap.
        • defense — multiply, hard cap at 4.0.
        • tracking — max() of the five. Homing does not stack; you either have it or you do not.
        • evasion — diminishing: 1 − Π(1 − eᵢ).

        Tag-derived defaults

        The top items are hand-rated. Everything else inherits from its tags, so nothing ever ships unrated.

        Tagoffenseaoetrackingother

        An item with no rating and no recognised tag falls back to quality alone: , applied to offense with everything else neutral.

        Turning a build into odds

        Each fight carries a weight vector over the five axes. Item ratings never encode boss-specific knowledge — the boss weights do all of that.

        The multiplicative axes go through log() so that neutral sits at zero and a halving mirrors a doubling. Tracking and evasion are already bounded 0–1 and are used directly. A fight's score is the weighted sum, and the chance of clearing it is sigmoid(slope × (score − threshold)). The run is the product of all thirteen.

        The thirteen fights are an interpretation. The source schema names Delirium and The Beast alongside a generic “bosses 1–12” ramp, which totals fourteen fights for a thing called 13-0. This build reads it as eleven floor bosses, then Delirium, then The Beast. If that reading is wrong, the weights below are the only thing that needs changing.
        #Fightoffaoetrkdefevathreshold

        Calibration

        slope and difficulty are not hand-picked constants. They are solved by bisection against two targets drawn from the schema, over a Monte Carlo of random five-item drafts:

        • the median draft should land at a 2–8% chance — higher and the achievement is worthless, lower and nobody finishes a run
        • the top 1% of drafts should read above 40% — the "I got a god roll" feeling has to be real

        The solver fits on one seed; tools/validate.mjs re-checks on a different one, so the fit has to generalise rather than memorise its own sample.

        What this is not

        Hitting a target distribution is not the same as being right. Every rating here was assigned by hand and calibrated so that random drafts land where the schema says they should — but no rating has been checked against how the game actually plays. Treat the number as a well-argued opinion with arithmetic on top, not as a measurement.

        The ratings live one-per-line in data/ratings.psv precisely so that disagreeing with one is a one-line diff. If a number looks wrong to you, it might be.

        Ratings are one person's opinion, argued in public. Everything else is arithmetic.
